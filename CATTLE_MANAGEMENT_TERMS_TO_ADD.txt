CATTLE MANAGEMENT TERMS PRESENT IN HERDSYNC BUT MISSING FROM THE PROVIDED GLOSSARY

Assessment scope: HerdSync cattle-management workflows, database schema, source code, and project documentation.
Note: Definitions below describe how each term is used in HerdSync. Add verified academic or regulatory citations before using them in a formal manuscript.

Admission Veterinary Review. A veterinary assessment of purchased or externally sourced cattle before release from quarantine and entry into the resident herd.

Administration Route. The method by which a medicine, vaccine, or treatment is delivered, such as oral, intramuscular, subcutaneous, intravenous, or topical administration.

Antibiotic Treatment. Use of an antimicrobial medicine to treat a diagnosed or suspected bacterial infection under an approved veterinary protocol, with treatment and withdrawal dates recorded.

Biosecurity Clearance. Documented confirmation that an animal has satisfied required quarantine, health-assessment, testing, treatment, and withdrawal conditions before joining the resident herd.

Breeding Eligibility. The determination that an animal can enter a breeding activity based on sex, age, health, Body Condition Score, reproductive status, quarantine status, treatment restrictions, and farm policy.

Breeding Soundness Examination. A structured evaluation of a breeding animal’s physical and reproductive fitness before it is approved for breeding.

Calf Care Plan. A scheduled set of post-birth care activities for a calf, including newborn assessment, identification, preventive care, and follow-up health checks.

Calf Vigor Score. A structured assessment of a newborn calf’s energy, alertness, movement, breathing, ability to stand, and ability to suckle after birth.

Calving Difficulty. The level of assistance required during birth, recorded in HerdSync as unassisted, minor assistance, major assistance, or caesarean.

Caesarean Section. Surgical delivery of a calf when vaginal delivery is unsafe or cannot be completed normally.

Clinical Follow-Up. A scheduled reassessment used to review an animal’s response to treatment, recovery status, unresolved symptoms, and need for additional care.

Conception Rate. The proportion of recorded breeding services that result in confirmed pregnancy during a defined period.

Deworming. Administration of an anthelmintic product to control internal parasites, with product, dose, route, administration date, next due date, and responsible personnel recorded.

Dystocia. Difficult or abnormal calving that requires assistance and may increase health risks for both dam and calf.

Estrus (Heat). The reproductive stage during which a female is sexually receptive and may be selected for natural breeding or artificial insemination.

Expected Calving Date. The estimated date on which a pregnant dam is expected to give birth, calculated from the recorded conception or breeding date and the expected cattle gestation period.

Gestation Checkup. A scheduled assessment during pregnancy used to record gestation stage, health condition, Body Condition Score, weight, clinical findings, veterinarian, and next checkup date.

Heat Observation. A recorded observation that a female displays signs of estrus, supporting the timing of breeding or artificial insemination.

Health Assessment. A structured evaluation of an animal’s current physical condition, clinical signs, diagnosis, health status, and need for treatment or monitoring.

Inbreeding Risk. The possibility that a proposed mating involves animals with a close genetic relationship, such as self, parent–offspring, or full siblings, which may increase inherited health and performance risks.

Lactation. The period during which a cow produces milk following calving.

Live-Calf Rate. The proportion of calves born alive among all recorded calf outcomes during a defined period.

Mating Compatibility. Evaluation of a proposed dam and sire pairing using pedigree, genetic defects, health status, reproductive fitness, and farm breeding policy.

Natural Breeding (Natural Service). A breeding method in which a sire directly mates with a dam, with the pair, service date, handler, and related observations recorded.

Neonatal Death. Death of a calf after being born alive during the neonatal period; HerdSync records the calf identity, survival details, observations, treatment, and cause when known.

Newborn Care. Immediate and early care following birth, including assessment of breathing, vigor, standing, suckling, colostrum access, abnormalities, identification, and follow-up needs.

Offspring Lineage. The recorded biological relationship connecting an animal to its sire, dam, and offspring for pedigree and breeding-history management.

Parasite Control. A planned herd-health program for preventing, monitoring, and treating internal or external parasites based on animal risk, clinical evidence, testing, season, grazing conditions, and veterinary guidance.

Pedigree. A documented record of an animal’s ancestry and descendants used to maintain parentage, assess relationships, and support breeding decisions.

Post-Breeding Care. Monitoring performed after breeding for return to heat, health changes, stress, nutrition, reproductive problems, and readiness for pregnancy confirmation.

Post-Calving Care. Assessment and care provided after birth to monitor dam recovery, calf vigor, nursing and colostrum intake, health complications, identification, and follow-up schedules.

Pregnancy Check. An assessment performed after breeding to determine whether a dam is pregnant, not pregnant, or requires continued monitoring.

Pregnancy Confirmation. A recorded positive pregnancy-check outcome that closes the breeding service as successful and starts active pregnancy monitoring.

Pregnancy Loss. Loss of a confirmed pregnancy before delivery, including abortion or other termination of gestation, recorded as an unsuccessful reproductive outcome requiring review.

Preventive Care. Planned health activities intended to reduce disease risk, including vaccination, deworming, vitamins, minerals, routine checkups, and other approved interventions.

Quarantine. Separation and monitoring of incoming, returning, or potentially exposed animals until required biosecurity and veterinary release conditions are met.

Reproductive Cycle. The connected sequence of heat observation, breeding service, pregnancy check, gestation monitoring, calving, and post-calving care.

Service Date. The date on which natural breeding or artificial insemination is performed and from which pregnancy-check and expected-calving schedules may be calculated.

Semen Traceability. Recording semen-related information such as batch number, straw identifier, sire code, source, technician, thaw details, and insemination site for an artificial insemination service.

Stillbirth. Birth of a calf that is dead at delivery; HerdSync records it as a calving outcome without creating an active livestock record for the calf.

Treatment Course. The complete prescribed period of medicine or clinical intervention, including start date, end date, dose, route, frequency, instructions, and follow-up requirements.

Treatment Withdrawal Status. The active, completed, or cleared state of a restriction linked to medicine use before an animal or its products may enter the food chain.

Vaccination. Administration of a vaccine to protect against a target disease, with product, dose, route, site, batch, expiry, administrator, and next due date recorded.

Veterinary Clearance. A veterinarian’s documented decision that an animal may be released from quarantine, treatment monitoring, or another health restriction after required conditions are satisfied.

Workflow Status. The operational state assigned to a cattle-management record, such as pending, due, overdue, open, resolved, successful, unsuccessful, confirmed, delivered, or completed.
